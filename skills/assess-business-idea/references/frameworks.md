# Analytical spine — frameworks applied every run

Each framework earns its place by catching a different way a business dies. All run at
every depth tier; depth changes how much evidence sits behind each. Founder claims
always enter as hypotheses to verify, never as facts.

## Triage screens (Phase 1)
- Real-Win-Worth-it (R-W-W): three blunt questions — is it real, can we win, is it
  worth it — used as a fast kill screen.
- Fast legality screen: is it legal in the recorded jurisdiction, does it need a licence
  the founder hasn't mentioned, is a regulator actively hostile there. A clear no is a
  fatal flaw.

## Demand and problem reality (catches: nobody actually wants this)
- Mom Test: did people pay or commit, or just say "nice idea"? Audits every founder
  demand claim for real signal versus politeness.
- Jobs To Be Done: what job is the customer hiring this to do? Reveals the real
  competition, which is often a spreadsheet or doing nothing.
- Sean Ellis signal: the "how would you feel if you could no longer use this" test as a
  forward validation target when a product already exists.

## Market size and structure (catches: too small or wrong shape)
- TAM/SAM/SOM, built bottom-up from evidence; the top-down number is flagged as the
  usual founder inflation.
- Porter Five Forces: supplier and buyer power, substitutes, ease of new entry,
  rivalry. Catches structurally unattractive markets that look big.
- Trends triangulation: multiple independent signals (see trends-sources.md), treated
  as directional only.

## Defensibility (catches: works, then gets copied to death)
- 7 Powers (Helmer): scale economies, network effects, counter-positioning, switching
  costs, branding, cornered resource, process power.
- Can't/Won't test: what stops a competitor copying the whole integrated strategy.

## Economics (catches: grows itself broke)
- Unit economics sanity: LTV to CAC (target at least 3:1), CAC payback under ~12
  months, contribution margin, burn to traction. A sanity pass, not a full model.
  Apply the 3:1 and 12 month thresholds only to recurring-revenue models. For
  one-off sales, hardware and marketplaces, state the per unit margin and describe
  the payback logic in words instead. Invent no new numeric thresholds for those
  models.

## Method — what to do next (catches: validated by opinion not evidence)
- Lean Startup riskiest-assumption test: name the single cheapest experiment to retire
  the biggest unknown.

## Comparative evidence (case studies + competitors)
- Analog/antilog case studies: find comparable ventures that succeeded or died and
  extract why. Who tried this shape before, and what happened.
- Competitor teardown: a background pull on each rival plus a positioning map.

## Additional critical dimensions
- Why-now thesis: what changed in the world to make this possible or inevitable now.
- Go-to-market and distribution: can you reach buyers affordably, and does the channel
  scale.
- Scalability: can it get big enough to matter (weighted heavily for investor, lightly
  for founder).
- Capital intensity and financing path: how much to reach the next proof point, and is
  that fundable now.
- Execution risk: how many independent things must all go right (modelled as a
  multiplier in scorecard.md, not an axis).

## Deliberately out of scope (avoid framework theater)
Detailed financial modelling (use the financial-model skill), founder background
due-diligence (use research-intel on people), full technical feasibility, ESG scoring
unless the user weights it.
